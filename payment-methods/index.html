The POLi question

POLi has been the default answer to "how do Kiwis deposit at online casinos" for fifteen years, and it is still on nearly every competing page. It was not available at any of the sixteen casinos we tested in 2026.

POLi the company is alive and operating in New Zealand. Its position actually improved when open banking rules took effect in December 2025 — it moved from the old screen-scraping approach, which banks warned customers against, onto bank-approved APIs with a set of partner banks. What changed is that it has been withdrawn from the gambling vertical. Payment providers routinely restrict gambling merchant categories, and POLi is no longer serving casino operators here.

The practical consequence: if a "best online pokies NZ" page is recommending POLi casinos to you, it has not been re-tested recently, and you should discount everything else on it accordingly.

Why NZD matters more than it sounds

A casino that only offers EUR or USD wallets applies a currency conversion on the way in and again on the way out. The spread is typically 2–3% each way, and it is priced into the exchange rate rather than charged as a visible fee, which is precisely why people miss it.

On a NZ$1,000 deposit and a NZ$1,000 withdrawal, that is roughly NZ$40–60 gone before a single spin. Over a year of ordinary play it is comfortably more than the RTP difference between the best and worst site on our list. All sixteen casinos we cover hold NZD balances, which is a hard requirement for inclusion.

Methods to avoid

Credit cards

Several New Zealand issuers decline gambling merchant category codes outright. Those that permit it commonly treat the transaction as a cash advance: a fee on the spot, interest accruing immediately with no grace period, and a higher rate than purchases. Beyond the cost, borrowing to gamble is the clearest single behavioural predictor of harm. Use a debit card.

Prepaid vouchers for large amounts

Neosurf, Flexepin and Paysafecard are genuinely useful as a hard spending cap — you cannot lose more than the voucher holds, which is a real harm-minimisation tool. They are deposit-only, so you will still need a verified second method to withdraw. Do not buy a large voucher expecting to cash out the same way.

Anyone else's payment method

Depositing with a partner's or parent's card breaches the terms at every operator we list and will be caught at verification, voiding winnings. There is no version of this that works.

Open banking: what replaced POLi

POLi worked by asking for your internet banking credentials and logging in on your behalf — screen scraping. New Zealand banks warned customers against it for years for exactly that reason, and it is why the service always sat awkwardly.

Open banking rules taking effect in December 2025 changed the model. Payment initiation now runs through bank-approved APIs with explicit consent and no credential sharing, which is a genuine improvement. POLi itself moved onto those rails with a set of partner banks.

What has not happened is any of that reaching gambling. Account-to-account services in New Zealand have not been adopted by offshore casino operators, most likely because acquiring gambling merchants requires relationships those providers have chosen not to pursue. Direct bank transfer remains the closest equivalent available to you, and it is slower.

Deposit limits as a payment decision

Worth stating plainly on a payments page: the method you choose is also a harm-minimisation decision.

  • A prepaid voucher is a hard cap. Neosurf, Flexepin and Paysafecard cannot lose you more than the voucher holds. Deposit-only, which is a limitation for withdrawals and a genuine protection for spending.
  • A debit card is bounded by your account. A credit card is not, which is most of why we recommend against it.
  • A bank-level gambling block covers every operator at once, including sites you have not joined. Several New Zealand banks offer it in-app, and it is the strongest control available online.
  • The deposit limit in account settings is the tool that works, because tightening is instant while loosening takes 24–72 hours.

Responsible gambling — play it safe, Kiwi

Gambling is entertainment, never a way to make money or escape stress. Set a deposit limit before you play rather than during a bad run, never chase a loss, and stop when it stops being fun. Every site we list offers deposit limits, loss limits, reality checks, time-outs and self-exclusion — use them.

If gambling is causing harm to you or someone you care about, free and confidential help is available in New Zealand, 24 hours a day. You do not need to be in crisis to call, and support is there for family and whānau too.
