The legal caps on a New Zealand pub pokie

Charlotte Wilson · Editor, Law & Regulation

I looked these up expecting to confirm something I already knew, and instead found the most useful fact on this entire site. Your local machine is legally forbidden from taking more than NZ$2.50 a spin or paying more than NZ$500 on one play. I have never seen that stated on a competing New Zealand pokies page.

Under the Gambling (Harm Prevention and Minimisation) Regulations 2004, a Class 4 gaming machine — the pokie in a pub, club or TAB venue — may not exceed:

Maximum stake per play
NZ$2.50
Maximum prize, single play
NZ$500
Maximum linked jackpot
NZ$1,000
Minimum age
18

These are not house policies. They are statutory limits, and they exist as harm minimisation: a machine that cannot take more than $2.50 a spin cannot take very much very fast. Class 4 gambling is explicitly classified by the Department of Internal Affairs as high-risk, high-turnover, and regulated accordingly.

Reading the table honestly

Charlotte Wilson · Editor, Law & Regulation

This is the part where an affiliate site is supposed to tell you online wins. It does on the top half of that table, and it loses on the bottom half, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Read both halves before you decide.

The online product is mathematically better for the player on return, stake freedom, prize ceiling and choice. Six percentage points of RTP is a large edge — on NZ$10,000 of turnover it is NZ$600 of difference. If your only question is "where does my money last longer", online wins clearly.

But look at the bottom half of the table. Every one of those rows describes a protection you give up. The $2.50 cap is not a limitation the pub imposes to annoy you; it is the mechanism that stops a bad half hour becoming a catastrophic one. Remove it and a player who was losing at most $150 an hour can lose that in three minutes.

The same goes for the regulator, the portable self-exclusion, and the fact that you have to physically go somewhere. Friction is protective. Online has none, and it is available at 2am when your judgement is at its worst.

The honest conclusion

Online pokies are a better product and a riskier one, and both of those things are true because the caps are gone. If you play online, you have to supply the limits the law used to supply for you. Set a deposit limit in account settings at signup — that is the closest online equivalent to the $2.50 cap, and it is the only one that works.

The pattern is stark once it is laid out. Online wins the two rows that depend on the operator choosing to build a tool, and loses every row that depends on something external constraining the operator. That is not an argument against playing online. It is an argument for setting the deposit limit at signup, because online you are supplying the caps yourself.

Where the money goes

One thing the pub genuinely does better. Class 4 gambling operates on a community-return model: non-club societies must distribute at least 40% of gross proceeds to authorised community purposes, and clubs at least 37% by licence condition. Roughly 23% goes to government plus a 1.5% problem gambling levy, and venues take up to 16%.

So when you lose $100 at a pub pokie, something in the order of $40 of the operator's profit is legally required to fund a sports club, a rescue helicopter or a community trust. When you lose $100 at an offshore online casino, it funds the operator and its licensing jurisdiction. Whether that changes your decision is up to you, but it is a real difference and almost nobody mentions it.

Pub pokies vs online pokies: frequently asked questions

What is the maximum bet on a pub pokie in New Zealand?

NZ$2.50 per play, set by the Gambling (Harm Prevention and Minimisation) Regulations 2004. The maximum prize for a single play is NZ$500 and the maximum linked jackpot is NZ$1,000. These are statutory limits rather than venue policy, and they exist specifically as harm minimisation.

Do online pokies pay more than pub pokies?

Yes, by roughly six percentage points of return. New Zealand Class 4 machines typically return around 90%, while the online pokies we recommend return 96% or more. On NZ$10,000 of turnover that difference is about NZ$600. Online also has no prize cap, where a pub machine cannot pay more than NZ$500 on a single play.

Are pub pokies safer than online pokies?

In terms of harm, yes. The NZ$2.50 stake cap bounds how fast you can lose, the venue has to be travelled to and closes, staff are trained to intervene, and Multi-Venue Exclusion lets you self-exclude across an entire region at once. Online has none of those brakes and is available at any hour. It is a better product and a riskier one for the same reason.

Where does pub pokie money go in New Zealand?

Non-club societies must distribute at least 40% of gross proceeds to authorised community purposes, and clubs at least 37% under licence conditions. Government takes about 23% plus a 1.5% problem gambling levy, and venues receive up to 16%. Money lost at an offshore online casino goes to the operator and its licensing jurisdiction instead.

Can I self-exclude from pokies in New Zealand?

Yes, and it works better in venues than online. Multi-Venue Exclusion lets you exclude yourself from every participating Class 4 venue in a region through a single request, and venue staff are trained to enforce it. Online self-exclusion is per-site and not portable between operators, so you have to repeat it everywhere you hold an account.

Responsible gambling — play it safe, Kiwi

Gambling is entertainment, never a way to make money or escape stress. Set a deposit limit before you play rather than during a bad run, never chase a loss, and stop when it stops being fun. Every site we list offers deposit limits, loss limits, reality checks, time-outs and self-exclusion — use them.

If gambling is causing harm to you or someone you care about, free and confidential help is available in New Zealand, 24 hours a day. You do not need to be in crisis to call, and support is there for family and whānau too.

  • Gambling Helpline Aotearoa — call 0800 654 655 or free text 8006
  • Problem Gambling Foundation — 0800 664 262, free counselling including kaupapa Māori, Pasifika and Asian services
  • Need to Talk — call or text 1737 to reach a trained counsellor any time
  • Safer Gambling Aotearoa — safergambling.org.nz
