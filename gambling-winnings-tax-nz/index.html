General information, not tax advice

This explains how New Zealand tax law generally treats gambling receipts. It is not advice on your circumstances. If the amounts are material, or if you gamble at scale, talk to a chartered accountant.

The general rule: winnings are a windfall

Charlotte Wilson · Editor, Law & Regulation

I fact-check every tax claim on this site, and this is the one readers most often arrive disbelieving. New Zealand really is more generous here than almost anywhere comparable. A New Zealander keeps a NZ$50,000 jackpot in full; an American in the same position would face federal withholding before the money reached them.

Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings received by a recreational player as a windfall gain rather than income. Windfall gains are not taxable in New Zealand. This means:

  • No income tax on your winnings, at any amount.
  • No threshold you cross by winning a lot at once. A NZ$50,000 jackpot is treated the same as a NZ$50 win.
  • No GST. You are not supplying anything.
  • No form to file and nothing to declare, if gambling is your only relevant activity.
  • No distinction between a pub pokie, an online pokie, a TAB bet or a Lotto ticket.

The logic is symmetrical: because gambling losses are not deductible against your other income, gambling gains are not assessable. IRD does not want to be in the business of subsidising losing punters, and the price of that is not taxing winning ones.

Exception one: gambling as a business

If you carry on gambling as a business, the receipts can be assessable income. IRD looks at the nature of the activity, not the size of a single win. The factors that matter:

  • Frequency and scale — is this continuous and substantial, or occasional?
  • System and organisation — do you operate a method, keep records, manage a bankroll as capital?
  • Intention to profit — is profit the object, or is entertainment?
  • Dependence — do you rely on it for a living?
  • Skill — is there a genuine edge available in the activity at all?

That last factor is why this exception essentially cannot apply to pokies. A pokie has a mathematically fixed negative expectation. There is no skill element and no available edge, so there is no basis on which spinning reels could constitute a profit-making business. The realistic candidates are professional poker players, sports arbitrage bettors and card counters — activities where a genuine edge exists.

Exception two: interest on your winnings

The win is not taxable. What it earns afterwards is. Put NZ$50,000 of pokies winnings in a term deposit and the interest is ordinary taxable income, subject to resident withholding tax like any other interest. Same for dividends if you invest it. The windfall character attaches to the receipt, not to the money forever.

Exception three: cryptoassets

Charlotte Wilson · Editor, Law & Regulation

This is the one I would flag hardest, and I have not found another New Zealand gambling page that covers it at all. The gambling win stays untaxed. What can be taxable is the crypto disposal sitting either side of it, and buying crypto specifically to fund a casino account looks a lot like acquiring property for the purpose of disposal.

This is the one that catches people, and no other New Zealand gambling guide covers it properly. Inland Revenue treats cryptoassets as property, not currency. Property has disposal rules that operate entirely independently of the gambling question.

Buying cryptoassets specifically in order to convert them into casino funds and back out again is, on its face, acquiring property with the purpose of disposal. The gambling winnings remain untaxed either way; the question is whether the movement in the crypto's value between acquisition and disposal is assessable.

The practical way to keep this simple: use a stablecoin such as USDT so there is effectively no movement to argue about, convert back to NZD promptly rather than holding, and keep a simple log of every acquisition and disposal with dates and NZD values. See crypto casinos.

Exception four: professional gambling-adjacent income

Income from gambling as distinct from winnings at gambling is ordinary income. Affiliate commission, streaming or content revenue, coaching, staking arrangements where you take a cut of someone else's play, and prize money for appearing rather than winning are all taxable. If you are being paid to gamble or to talk about gambling, that is business income regardless of how your own bets go.

Bank a large win in your own name

No tax is due, but a large deposit into a New Zealand bank account will trigger anti-money-laundering questions under the AML/CFT Act. That is your bank's obligation, not IRD's. Keep the casino's withdrawal confirmation and transaction history so you can evidence the source immediately. Withdrawing to an account in your own name, matching your casino account name, avoids most of the friction.

Tax on gambling winnings NZ: frequently asked questions

Do you pay tax on pokies winnings in New Zealand?

No, not as a recreational player. Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings as a windfall gain rather than income, and windfall gains are not taxable in New Zealand. There is no threshold, no form to file and no GST, and it makes no difference whether the win came from a pub pokie, an online pokie, the TAB or Lotto.

Is there GST on gambling winnings?

Not for the player. GST applies to supplies of goods and services, and winning a bet is not a supply you have made. Operators have their own GST and gambling duty obligations, including the offshore gambling duty that rose from 12% to 16% in October 2025, but none of that is charged to your winnings.

When does gambling become taxable in New Zealand?

When it amounts to carrying on a business. IRD looks at frequency, scale, system, intention to profit, dependence on it for income, and whether a genuine edge exists in the activity. That last point means pokies effectively cannot qualify, since they carry a fixed negative expectation and no skill element. The realistic candidates are professional poker players, sports arbitrage bettors and card counters.

Do I pay tax on crypto casino winnings in NZ?

Not on the gambling win itself, which remains a windfall. But Inland Revenue treats cryptoassets as property rather than currency, so disposing of crypto back to New Zealand dollars can be taxable if you acquired it with the purpose of disposal. Buying crypto specifically to fund a casino account and convert back arguably indicates exactly that purpose. Use a stablecoin, convert promptly, and keep records.

Do I have to declare a large gambling win to IRD?

No, if gambling is your only relevant activity, since there is nothing assessable to declare. You may still face questions from your bank when a large sum arrives, because anti-money-laundering rules require them to understand the source of funds. Keep the casino's withdrawal confirmation and transaction history so you can answer immediately.

Are gambling losses deductible in New Zealand?

No. Recreational gambling losses cannot be deducted against your other income, which is the mirror image of winnings not being assessable. If gambling did constitute a business for you, losses would follow the same rules as any other business, but that scenario is narrow and does not arise for pokies play.
