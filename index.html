How online pokies actually work

A pokie is a random number generator with a paytable wrapped around it. Understanding three numbers is enough to make you a better-informed player than 95% of the people spinning next to you.

RNG: every spin is independent

The random number generator is cycling continuously whether or not anyone is playing. When you press spin, it takes the value at that instant and maps it to a reel position. There is no memory between spins. A machine is not "due", a title that has just paid is not "cold", and the time of day makes no difference. Any strategy built on the opposite belief is built on nothing.

RTP: the long-run return

Return to player is the share of all wagers a title returns over millions of spins. A 96.5% pokie returns NZ$96.50 per NZ$100 staked across its whole population of players over its lifetime — not across your Saturday night. Over a single session, variance dominates completely.

The detail that matters and that operators rarely volunteer: many titles ship in multiple RTP configurations, and the site chooses which one to deploy. The same game can run at 96.5% at one casino and 94.0% at another. This is why we sample rather than quote press releases — our lead reviewer Nikau Te Aho records the configured return served to a New Zealand IP on 40 fixed titles at every site we list. The medians are in the table above and on highest RTP pokies.

Volatility: how the return arrives

Two pokies can both return 96% and feel nothing alike. Low volatility pays small and often; your balance drifts down slowly and you play a long time on NZ$20. High volatility pays rarely and large; most sessions end at zero and a small number end at 500×. Volatility is not a quality rating — it is a match to your bankroll and your temperament. On NZ$20 with 20-cent spins, a high-volatility title will usually be over before the bonus round triggers.

The one edge available to you

You cannot influence the outcome of a spin. You can choose a 96.8% title over a 94.2% one, and refuse bonus terms that make cashing out impossible. Those two decisions are the entire strategic surface of pokies. Everything else sold as "system" is noise.

  3. Complete verification now, not later

    Upload your passport or driver licence and a recent bank or power statement the day you sign up. Nearly every "the casino won't pay me" story we investigate is really a player who hit verification for the first time while trying to withdraw. Do it while you are not waiting on money.

  4. Decide about the bonus deliberately

Why is my withdrawal pending?

Nikau Te Aho · Lead Reviewer

I spent five years on the other side of this. I worked the queue where withdrawals get held, so I can tell you what is actually happening when your cashout sits there saying pending and nobody explains why. It is almost never what players assume, and in four of these five cases there is something specific you can do about it today.

This is one of the most-searched pokies questions in New Zealand and almost nobody answers it properly. "Pending" is not one thing. It is five different states wearing the same label, and the right response is different for each.

1. The reversal window

Many casinos hold withdrawals for 12–48 hours during which you can cancel and return the money to your balance. This exists to encourage you to gamble it back, and it works. If your site has a "cancel withdrawal" button, treat that as a hostile design feature: request the cashout and then close the tab.

2. Verification is incomplete

The most common cause. The site is waiting on ID, proof of address, or proof of the payment method. Frequently nobody tells you — the request simply sits. Open live chat and ask directly what document is outstanding.

3. Bonus wagering is not finished

If any part of your balance is bonus-derived and the playthrough is incomplete, the withdrawal will not process. Check the bonus tracker in your account, not the main balance figure.

4. Manual review over a threshold

Amounts over roughly NZ$2,000–3,000 often route to a human. That reviewer works business hours in a European or Asian timezone, so a Friday-night New Zealand request can genuinely sit until Monday. This is normal, not a red flag.

5. Source-of-funds check

On large or unusual wins, anti-money-laundering rules can require proof of where your deposit money came from — payslips or bank statements. Intrusive, legitimate, and unavoidable at a licensed operator.

When to escalate: if a withdrawal has been pending more than 72 hours with verification complete and no bonus attached, and support cannot name the specific reason, that is the point to start a formal complaint. Our complaints and disputes guide sets out exactly how, with the escalation addresses for each licensor.

What happens if an online casino goes bust?

Almost no New Zealand-facing guide raises this, and it is the single largest structural risk of playing at an offshore site. Your balance at an online casino is not a bank deposit. There is no New Zealand deposit guarantee behind it, and none of the offshore licensors that cover the sites Kiwis can access — Curaçao, Anjouan, Tobique — operate a compensation scheme comparable to the UK or Malta.

Some operators hold player funds in accounts segregated from operating capital, meaning your balance is not available to general creditors if the company fails. Many do not, and very few say either way in plain language. Where segregation exists it is usually stated in the terms under a heading like "player funds" or "client money".

The practical consequence is simple and worth internalising: an online casino balance is not savings. Withdraw winnings promptly rather than leaving a float on the site, keep your working balance to what you would be prepared to lose to an insolvency as well as to the games, and prefer operators with high ceilings, because a high ceiling is what lets you actually get money out quickly when you decide to.

How to complain about an online casino from New Zealand

Nikau Te Aho · Lead Reviewer

I have watched complaints get dismissed for one reason more than any other: the player escalated before exhausting the operator's own process, so the licensor threw it out on procedure without ever looking at the merits. The order below matters more than the wording. Do not skip a stage.

You have less protection than you would at a New Zealand-regulated business, but you are not without recourse. The route works in four stages and skipping ahead wastes your time.

  1. Formal internal complaint, in writing

    Email — never only live chat — using the word "complaint" explicitly. State your account ID, the amount, dates, and what you want. Licensed operators run a mandatory internal complaints procedure with a defined response window, typically 14 days. Keep every reply.

  2. Escalate to the licensor

    Once internal complaints are exhausted or the window lapses, go to the regulator on the licence. The Curaçao Gaming Authority took over from the old master-licence system and now accepts player complaints directly; Anjouan and Tobique have their own forms. The licence number is in the site footer — you will need it.

  3. Use an independent ADR or mediation service

Pokies bonuses: what the wagering number really costs

Nikau Te Aho · Lead Reviewer

My colleague Charlotte read all sixteen sets of bonus terms in full for this section, and I ran the arithmetic on what each one would actually cost to clear. I declined the bonus at four of the sixteen sites I signed up to, and I would do the same again. Being able to withdraw whenever I liked was worth more to me than the extra spins.

The headline figure on a welcome offer is the least informative number in the whole promotion. Four things behind it decide whether the offer is worth taking.

Wagering multiplier and base

40× on bonus only after a NZ$100 deposit with a NZ$100 bonus means NZ$4,000 of turnover. The same 40× on deposit plus bonus means NZ$8,000. Identical headline, double the obligation. Always find which base applies.

Game weighting

Pokies usually count 100% toward wagering. Live blackjack and roulette often count 10% or nothing. If you intend to play tables, a pokies-weighted bonus is close to worthless to you.

Maximum bet while wagering

Typically NZ$5–8 per spin. Exceed it once, even accidentally on autoplay, and the operator can void the entire bonus and everything won from it. This is the single most common way players lose a legitimate win.

Win cap and expiry

No-deposit offers usually cap what you can withdraw — often NZ$100 regardless of what you win. Most bonuses expire in 7–30 days. An offer you cannot clear in the window is not value.

Our rule after testing sixteen welcome packages: a smaller bonus with 25–35× wagering on bonus only is worth more than a headline package at 50× on deposit plus bonus. We price every offer this way on our casino bonuses page, and explain the arithmetic in full under wagering requirements explained.

Do you pay tax on pokies winnings in New Zealand?

For a recreational player, no. Inland Revenue treats gambling winnings as a windfall rather than income. There is no threshold you cross by winning a large amount on one night, no form to file, and no GST on your winnings. A NZ$50,000 jackpot is yours in full.

Two qualifications. First, if you gamble as a business — systematically, at scale, as your income — IRD can assess that as taxable, judged on the nature of the activity rather than the amount won. Pokies tournaments

Uncontested ground in New Zealand — almost no competing guide covers tournaments, and they are one of the few ways to add value to spins you were making anyway. The format is simple: over a set window, players compete on a leaderboard measured by biggest single win multiplier, or by total wins, on a specified set of titles. Prizes are split across the top finishers.

Three things to check before entering, because the difference between them is the difference between real value and none:

  • Cash or bonus credit? A NZ$500 prize paid as bonus credit at 40× wagering is worth a fraction of NZ$500 cash. Spino pays most leaderboard prizes in cash, which is why it takes our tournament pick.
  • Multiplier-based or turnover-based? Multiplier leaderboards (biggest win relative to stake) are winnable on small stakes and are the only format worth a casual player's time. Turnover leaderboards simply reward whoever gambles most, and you will not out-stake a high roller.
  • Minimum qualifying spin. If it is NZ$1 and you normally play at NZ$0.20, entering means quintupling your stake to compete. That cost is rarely worth the prize.

Nothing but the word. "Pokies" is the New Zealand and Australian term for what the rest of the world calls slot machines or slots, short for poker machines. What is volatility and which should I choose?

Volatility describes how a pokie's return arrives. Low volatility pays small amounts frequently, so your balance declines slowly and a NZ$20 deposit lasts a long time. High volatility pays rarely but large, so most sessions end at zero and a few end at hundreds of times your stake. It is not a quality measure but a match to your bankroll. On NZ$20 at 20 cents a spin, a high-volatility title will usually be over before the bonus round triggers.
