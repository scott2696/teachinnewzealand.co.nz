Check any pokie's real RTP in thirty seconds

  1. Open the game and find the information button

    An "i", a burger icon, or inside the settings cog — usually bottom-left or bottom-right of the game frame.

  2. Go to the last page of the paytable

    The RTP statement is almost always in the final section with the rules text and maximum win.

  3. Compare against the studio's published figure

    Search the title plus the studio name plus "RTP". A material gap means a reduced build.

  4. If no RTP is displayed anywhere, treat it as a warning

    An operator running standard configurations has no reason to hide the number.

RTP, hit frequency and volatility are three different things

Nikau Te Aho · Lead Reviewer

These get used interchangeably and they measure completely different things. Understanding the difference is the whole of what makes a player informed rather than superstitious.

MeasureWhat it tells youTypical rangeAffectsPublished?
RTPShare of all wagers returned over millions of spins94–98%Your long-run expected lossYes, in the game info panel
Hit frequencyProportion of spins producing any win at all15–40%How the session feels moment to momentRarely
VolatilityHow the return is distributed between small and large winsLow to very highWhether your bankroll survives to the bonus roundUsually as a rating out of 5
Max winCapped ceiling as a multiple of stake500×–150,000×The top end, and nothing elseYes

The one nobody publishes is bonus frequency, and it is the number that decides whether a high-volatility title is playable on your bankroll. A game triggering its bonus once in 300 spins needs roughly 300 spins of bankroll to give you a fair shot. At NZ$0.20 a spin that is NZ$60. At NZ$1 it is NZ$300.

Mega Joker at 99% and Jackpot 6000 at 98.8% top every "highest RTP slots" list on the internet, almost always without the qualification that makes those numbers real.

How RTP is certified, and by whom

RTP is not a claim a studio makes about itself. It is a figure verified by an independent testing laboratory against the game's actual maths model, usually over hundreds of millions of simulated spins.

LabWhat it verifiesWho commissions it
iTech LabsRNG randomness and that the declared RTP matches the maths modelThe studio, per title and per configuration
GLIFull game and platform certification to jurisdictional standardsStudio and platform; required by most licensors
eCOGRARNG plus operator-level payout reporting and dispute handlingThe operator
BMM TestlabsGame maths and RNG for supplier certificationThe studio

The critical detail: each configuration is certified separately. A 96.5% build and a 94.5% build of the same title are both legitimately certified. Neither is rigged. The operator selects which to license, and the only place that choice is disclosed is the game's information panel.

RTP, house edge and hold: three names for related things

TermDefinitionWorked example
RTPShare of total wagers returned to players over the game's lifetime96% RTP returns NZ$96 per NZ$100 staked, across all players
House edgeThe inverse. What the operator retains96% RTP is a 4% house edge
HoldWhat the operator actually kept in practice, after real player behaviourUsually higher than house edge, because players recycle winnings
Expected lossHouse edge applied to your turnover, not your depositNZ$4,000 of turnover at 4% is NZ$160 expected, from a NZ$100 deposit recycled many times

That last row is the one people get wrong. Your expected loss scales with turnover, not with what you deposited. A NZ$100 deposit played through forty times generates NZ$4,000 of turnover and about NZ$160 of expected loss — which is why you cannot lose more than you deposited, but you will usually lose all of it given enough spins.

The myths worth discarding

  • "This pokie is due." The RNG has no memory. A title that has not paid in 400 spins has exactly the same chance on spin 401 as on spin 1.
  • "Higher stakes trigger better outcomes." Outcomes are stake-independent on every certified title. What changes is the size of the win, not its likelihood. The exception is the Supermeter mode noted above, where the RTP genuinely differs by stake and the game says so.
  • "Casinos tighten games at busy times." The operator does not hold the code and cannot adjust a live title. It can only choose which certified build to license, and that choice is set in advance, not adjusted by traffic.
  • "Autoplay pays worse than manual spins." Identical. The RNG is sampled at the moment the spin executes regardless of what triggered it.
  • "A big win means the game is now cold." Same fallacy as "due", in the opposite direction.

Highest RTP pokies: frequently asked questions

What is the highest RTP pokie available in New Zealand?

Mega Joker at 99% and Jackpot 6000 at 98.8%, both NetEnt, but only in Supermeter mode at maximum stake. In ordinary play the practical leaders are Blood Suckers at 98%, Starmania at 97.9%, and White Rabbit Megaways at 97.7%, all widely available and all reaching those figures in normal play.

Do casinos change the RTP of pokies?

They choose between configurations the studio has already certified. Many titles ship at 96.5%, 94.5% and sometimes 92%, and the operator decides which build to deploy. It is legal and disclosed only in the game's information panel. Across our fixed 40-title sample, the worst site ran reduced builds on 6 of 40 titles and the best ran none.

How much difference does RTP actually make?

On NZ$10,000 of turnover, which a NZ$20-a-week player reaches in a year, the gap between a 96.4% site and a 95.8% site is about NZ$60. Not dramatic, but it is free to capture since the games are identical. On individual titles the effect is larger: 98% versus 94% halves your expected loss rate.

Is a high RTP pokie easier to win on?

It loses you money more slowly over the long run, which is not the same thing. RTP says nothing about how the return is distributed. A 98% low-volatility title pays small amounts often; a 96.5% high-volatility title pays rarely and enormously. For a single session, volatility affects your experience far more than RTP does.

What is the highest RTP pokie you can actually play in NZ?

In ordinary play, Blood Suckers at 98%, Starmania at 97.9% and White Rabbit Megaways at 97.7%. Mega Joker at 99% and Jackpot 6000 at 98.8% are quoted more often but only reach those figures in Supermeter mode at maximum stake — in base mode at low stakes the return is closer to 90%.

Is a 96% RTP pokie good?

It is the market standard and perfectly reasonable. Below 95% is poor value when identical games sit at 96.5% one click away. Above 97% is genuinely good and worth seeking out. The more important question is which build the casino has deployed, since many titles ship at 96.5%, 94.5% and sometimes 92%.

How do I check a pokie's real RTP?

Open the game, find the information button — usually an 'i' or burger icon in the game frame — and go to the last page of the paytable. The RTP statement is almost always there with the rules text. Compare it against the studio's published figure; a material gap means the operator has deployed a reduced build.

Do all casinos serve the same RTP for the same game?

No, and this is the point most guides miss. Studios certify multiple configurations and the operator chooses which to deploy. Across 440 checks at eleven casinos we found 26 titles running below the studio standard. The best site ran none of forty; the worst ran six. Nothing on either casino tells a player which one they have walked into.

Does higher RTP mean I win more often?

No. RTP is long-run return; hit frequency is how often any win lands, and volatility is how the return is distributed. A 98% low-volatility title pays small amounts frequently while a 96.4% very-high-volatility title pays almost nothing most sessions and occasionally pays 150,000 times stake. Both are 'high RTP'.

What is bonus frequency and why does it matter?

The average number of spins between free-spin triggers, typically 1 in 90 to 1 in 400. Almost no studio publishes it, and it is the number that decides whether a high-volatility title is playable on your bankroll. A game triggering once in 300 spins needs roughly 300 spins of bankroll — NZ$60 at NZ$0.20 a spin, NZ$300 at NZ$1.

Are high RTP pokies excluded from bonuses?

Frequently, yes. Operators commonly exclude the highest-return titles from bonus wagering, or weight them below 100%, because clearing a requirement on a 98% game costs the casino far less than on a 96% one. Check the excluded games list in the bonus terms before you start.

Which studios serve the highest returns?

NetEnt's back catalogue is strongest, with Blood Suckers at 98% and several titles above 96.5%. Thunderkick reaches 98.5% on 1429 Uncharted Seas. Big Time Gaming holds the highest Megaways return at 97.7%. Games Global has the widest spread because its progressive network drags the low end to 88%.
