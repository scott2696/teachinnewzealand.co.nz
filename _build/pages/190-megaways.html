What Megaways actually does

Big Time Gaming's mechanic, licensed to dozens of other studios. On each spin, every reel loads a variable number of symbols — typically two to seven. Wins pay for matching symbols on adjacent reels from the left, regardless of position. Six reels at seven symbols each gives 7⁶ = 117,649 ways.

The critical thing to understand: the maximum ways figure is a marketing number, not a typical one. All six reels loading seven symbols simultaneously is rare. Most spins run at a few thousand ways. And more ways means each individual way pays proportionally less — the total return is unchanged, just redistributed.

Nearly all Megaways titles pair the mechanic with cascading reels: winning symbols are removed, new ones drop in, and the process repeats. In free spins a win multiplier usually increments with each cascade and does not reset, which is where the enormous wins come from and why the base game feels so tight by comparison.

Bankroll maths for Megaways

These titles need roughly 200–300 spins to give the free-spins round a fair chance to trigger. On NZ$20 that means spinning at NZ$0.10, not NZ$1. Playing Megaways at a stake your bankroll cannot sustain for 200 spins means you will almost always run out before the part of the game that pays.

Where the money actually is

On most Megaways titles the base game is deliberately thin and the free-spins round carries the return. That is the design. It also means the practical question is not "what is the RTP" but "how many spins until the bonus triggers, and can my bankroll survive that".

Feature Drop is the one worth understanding

On White Rabbit and a handful of others, collected scatters persist across spins so the bonus becomes progressively more likely rather than resetting. It converts an unbounded wait into a bounded one, which is genuinely better for a limited bankroll — you can see how close you are. It is also the reason White Rabbit's 97.7% is the highest Megaways return available.

Who licenses the mechanic

Big Time Gaming invented Megaways and licenses it. The originals — Bonanza, White Rabbit, Extra Chilli, Danger High Voltage — remain the reference implementations, and not every lobby carries them.

Licensees producing their own Megaways titles include Pragmatic Play, Red Tiger, Blueprint Gaming, iSoftBet, Relax Gaming, ReelPlay, BGaming and Kalamba. Quality varies considerably: a licensed Megaways title is not automatically as well-tuned as a Big Time Gaming original, and several licensees ship reduced-RTP builds more readily. Check the information panel, and if a lobby carries no BTG originals at all, that tells you something about its licensing budget.

Playing Megaways sensibly

Set the stake from the bankroll, not the other way round

Divide your bankroll by 250. That is your stake. NZ$50 gives you NZ$0.20 a spin; NZ$20 gives you NZ$0.10. Anything higher and you are paying for a feature you will not reach.

Prefer Feature Drop titles on a small bankroll

White Rabbit and its relatives keep collected scatters between spins, so the bonus becomes progressively more likely rather than resetting. It converts an unbounded wait into a bounded one, which is exactly what a limited bankroll needs.

Treat the gamble-the-free-spins feature as optional

Extra Chilli lets you risk awarded spins to win more. It raises variance sharply on a format that is already high variance. Declining it is not leaving value on the table — the RTP is the same either way.

Check whether the lobby carries the originals

Big Time Gaming's own titles are the reference implementations. A lobby with no BTG originals is carrying licensed imitations only, which vary considerably in tuning and are more likely to ship reduced-RTP builds.
