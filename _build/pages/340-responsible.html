What we think about this

We publish a site that recommends places to gamble, and we take commission when readers sign up. Being straightforward about the risk is not a disclaimer we bolt on at the bottom — it is the condition on which the rest of the site is honest.

Pokies are engineered to be compelling. Near-miss animations, celebratory sounds on losing spins, the pacing of the reels: all of it is designed by people who are very good at their jobs. Finding it absorbing is the product working as intended, not a weakness in you. And online, the brakes New Zealand law puts on a pub machine — the $2.50 stake cap, the $500 prize cap, having to physically go somewhere that closes — are all gone.

That means the limits have to come from you, set before you start rather than during a bad run.

The tools that actually work

Deposit limits

The single most effective tool available. Set a daily, weekly or monthly cap in account settings at signup. Tightening takes effect immediately; loosening takes 24 to 72 hours at any reputable operator — that delay is the entire point, because it puts a cooling-off period between the impulse and the money.

Loss limits

Caps net losses rather than deposits, which catches the pattern where you deposit, win, lose it back and deposit again. Available at every site we list.

Session limits and reality checks

A time cap, or a periodic pop-up showing elapsed time and net position. Sounds trivial; is not. Losing track of time is one of the most reliable markers of a session going wrong.

Time-out

A short break of 24 hours to six weeks. Lower friction than self-exclusion and useful when you simply need to stop for a while.

Self-exclusion

Blocks your account for six months to permanently, and is binding on the operator. The limitation is that it is per-site and not portable — you have to do it everywhere you hold an account.

Bank-level gambling blocks

Several New Zealand banks let you block gambling merchant categories on your card from within the app. This is the most durable option because it covers every operator at once, including ones you have not signed up to yet.

The gap between venues and online

In a New Zealand pub or club, Multi-Venue Exclusion lets you exclude yourself from every participating Class 4 venue in a region through a single request, and staff are trained to enforce it. There is no online equivalent. Online self-exclusion is per-operator and stops at that operator's door. If you need a broad block, the bank-level gambling block is the closest online equivalent, and it is worth setting up.

Signs worth taking seriously

None of these alone means you have a problem. Several together is worth a free, confidential phone call.

  • Gambling with money set aside for rent, bills, food or debt repayments.
  • Borrowing to gamble — credit cards, buy-now-pay-later, family, payday lenders. This is the clearest single predictor of harm.
  • Chasing losses: increasing stakes or session length specifically to recover what you have lost.
  • Hiding it. Deleting history, minimising windows, being vague with a partner about amounts.
  • Losing track of time or of how much has gone through the account.
  • Gambling to change how you feel — to escape stress, boredom, grief or anxiety — rather than for entertainment.
  • Feeling irritable, restless or low when you cannot play.
  • Someone close to you has said something about it. This one is easy to dismiss and worth weighting heavily.

Practical rules that help

  1. Decide the amount before you open the site

    An entertainment budget you would be equally willing to spend on a night out. Once it is gone, the session is over — not "one more deposit".

  2. Set the deposit limit while you are calm

    At signup, before there is any money at stake. The limit set on a Tuesday afternoon is the one that protects you at 1am on a Saturday.

  3. Never chase

    The single most reliable way a manageable evening becomes an unmanageable one. A loss is spent. Increasing stakes to recover it increases the size of the eventual loss, not the chance of recovery.

  4. Never gamble with borrowed money

    Not credit cards, not buy-now-pay-later, not a loan from family. The maths is bad and the psychology is worse.

  5. Withdraw winnings rather than leaving a float

    A balance left on a site gets played back. That is the commercial logic of the reversal window.

  6. Do not play to fix a feeling

    Gambling to escape stress, boredom or grief is the pattern most strongly associated with harm. If that is why you are opening the site, do something else.

  7. Take the break seriously

    If a session is going badly, stop. A time-out is a tool, not an admission of anything.

Help for family and whānau

If someone you care about is gambling harmfully, you can call every service on this page yourself. You do not need their permission and you do not need them to agree there is a problem. The Problem Gambling Foundation and the Gambling Helpline both provide support specifically for family members, including practical guidance on protecting shared finances and on how to raise it without a confrontation.

Harm from gambling affects more people than the person gambling, and the support system in New Zealand is built with that in mind.

Responsible gambling: frequently asked questions

Where can I get free gambling help in New Zealand?

The Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655 or free text 8006, available 24 hours a day. The Problem Gambling Foundation on 0800 664 262 offers free face-to-face and online counselling nationwide including kaupapa Maori, Pasifika and Asian services. Need to Talk on 1737 connects you to a trained counsellor by call or text any time. All are free and confidential, and all support family and whanau as well as the person gambling.

Through each operator's account settings, where self-exclusion runs from six months to permanent and is binding on that operator. The limitation is that it is per-site and not portable, so you must repeat it everywhere you hold an account. A bank-level gambling block, offered by several New Zealand banks within their apps, covers every operator at once including ones you have not signed up to, and is the more durable option.

Do deposit limits actually work?

They are the most effective single tool available, because of the asymmetry built into them. Tightening a limit takes effect immediately while loosening one takes 24 to 72 hours at any reputable operator. That delay puts a cooling-off period between an impulse and the money, which is exactly when protection is needed. Set them at signup while you are calm, not during a session.

How do I know if I have a gambling problem?

The markers worth taking seriously are gambling with money meant for rent or bills, borrowing to gamble, chasing losses, hiding it from people close to you, losing track of time or amounts, and gambling to change how you feel rather than for entertainment. If someone close to you has raised it, weight that heavily. You do not need certainty or a diagnosis to make a free confidential call to 0800 654 655.

Yes. Every service on this page supports family and whanau directly, and you do not need the person's permission or agreement that a problem exists.
