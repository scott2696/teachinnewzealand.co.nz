The withdrawal timing protocol

  1. Open a real account in a real name

    Registered from New Zealand, with genuine details matching the ID we will submit. No press accounts, no operator-provided test accounts.

  2. Complete verification immediately

    Photo ID and proof of address on day one, so that later timings measure the cashier rather than the document queue.

  3. Deposit our own New Zealand dollars

    Typically NZ$100–300, on our own payment methods. We do not accept operator-funded balances for testing.

  4. Play a real session

    Enough turnover to be a normal customer rather than a deposit-and-withdraw pattern, which several operators flag.

  5. Request withdrawals on three rails

    Crypto, e-wallet and card where supported, at least once each, on weekday mornings NZ time to control for weekend review queues.

  6. Record request time and funds-available time

    Not the operator's "processed" notification. The clock stops when the money is spendable.

  7. Repeat

    At least three withdrawals per operator. A single fast result is not a payout speed; consistency is.

The RTP sample

Forty titles, the same forty at every operator, chosen to span studios and mechanics: Pragmatic Play, Play'n GO, NetEnt, Big Time Gaming, Hacksaw, Nolimit City, Relax, BGaming and Games Global, across classic, video, Megaways, cluster and jackpot formats.

For each, we open the game from a New Zealand IP address and read the configured RTP from the information panel — not the studio's published figure. We record the median across the forty and count how many are running a reduced configuration relative to the studio standard. This is what lets us say a site serves 96.4% rather than repeating a press release.

What disqualifies an operator

Any one of these removes a site from our rankings regardless of its score elsewhere:

  • A withdrawal we made ourselves exceeded five business days with verification complete, no bonus attached, and no specific reason given by support.
  • Terms permit voiding winnings on undefined grounds — clauses about "irregular play" or "advantage play" with no definition. A numeric max-bet rule is fine; a discretionary clause is not.
  • Median served RTP below 95% across the fixed sample, indicating systematic deployment of reduced-return builds.
  • No verifiable licence, or a footer licence number that does not resolve on the licensor's public register.
  • Responsible gambling tools missing or hidden. Deposit limits, loss limits, session limits and self-exclusion must be present and reachable from account settings.
  • No NZD wallet, which costs New Zealand players 2–3% in conversion spread each way.
  • A substantiated non-payment complaint from a reader, with documentation, that the operator cannot answer.

How commission is kept out

We are an affiliate site. Several operators listed here pay us a share of revenue when a reader opens an account through our link. This is disclosed at the top of every page and we think it is a legitimate way to fund testing that costs real money.

Three things keep it out of the rankings:

  • Scores are computed from the measured inputs above. Commercial terms are not one of them and have no field in our data.
  • The person doing the testing does not negotiate the commercial terms, and the person who does has no ability to change a score.
  • We publish sites we are not paid by where they are the right answer. Our sports betting page recommends TAB NZ, from whom we receive nothing, because it is the only lawful option — and declines to link the offshore sportsbooks that would pay us.

If you believe a ranking is commercially influenced, tell us and we will show you the scoring inputs for that operator.

How often we re-test

Payout times and ceilings are re-verified quarterly and after any reader report. RTP samples are re-run twice a year, since operators change configurations infrequently but do change them. Bonus terms are checked monthly, because they change constantly. Legal and regulatory content is reviewed whenever the Department of Internal Affairs publishes an update, which through the current licensing programme has meant roughly monthly.

Every page carries the date it was last updated at the top, next to the author. If a figure looks stale, it is because that page has not been re-tested since that date — we would rather show you an honest date than a rolling one.

Corrections

We correct errors in place and say what changed. If you find one, email us with the page and the issue. We do not quietly edit factual claims that readers may have relied on; substantive corrections are noted on the page.
