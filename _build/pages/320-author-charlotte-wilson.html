Background

Charlotte Wilson read law at Te Herenga Waka—Victoria University of Wellington and spent four years covering regulatory affairs before moving into gambling publishing. She joined Pokies Kiwi to handle the part of this subject that most affiliate sites get wrong: what the law actually says, as opposed to what is convenient to claim.

What she does here

  • Fact-checks every legal, tax and licensing claim on this site before publication. Nothing about the Gambling Act 2003, the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026, Inland Revenue's treatment of winnings or the TAB NZ betting position goes live without her sign-off.
  • Reads the complete terms and conditions on every bonus we publish — all sixteen operators, in full, including the clauses that permit voiding winnings. The bonus pricing on this site comes out of that reading.
  • Tracks the Department of Internal Affairs licensing programme through its expression-of-interest, auction and application stages, and updates our law page as milestones move.
  • Wrote the escalation route on our complaints and disputes page, including which licensors will actually take a player complaint and what evidence each stage needs.

The position she takes on contested law

Where the legal position is genuinely uncertain — and in a market mid-way through its first licensing round, several things are — she says so in the text. This site does not present contested readings as settled, and it does not soften an inconvenient conclusion. The clearest example is our online betting page, which tells readers that the offshore sportsbooks we could earn commission from cannot lawfully accept their bets.

Areas of expertise

New Zealand gambling law, the Gambling Act 2003, the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 and the DIA licensing regime, Class 4 regulation, the Racing Industry Act and the TAB NZ monopoly, Inland Revenue treatment of windfall gains and cryptoassets, bonus terms and consumer contract fairness, and responsible gambling regulation.

A note on legal content

Everything Charlotte writes here is general information about how New Zealand law applies to consumers, sourced to primary materials and linked. It is not legal advice on anyone's particular circumstances, and it is not a substitute for talking to a lawyer or an accountant where the amounts are material.
