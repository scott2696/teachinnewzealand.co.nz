The calculation

Three inputs: the multiplier, the base it applies to, and the RTP of what you will play.

Turnover required = multiplier × base
Expected cost = turnover × (1 − RTP)

An important qualification

"Expected cost" is the house edge on turnover you were going to generate anyway if you intended to play that much. If you were going to stake NZ$4,000 this month regardless, a 40× bonus-only offer is free money on top. If you were going to stake NZ$200, the bonus is what makes you stake NZ$4,000 — and that is the entire commercial point of it.

What each site actually asks of you

The three terms that void wins

Maximum bet while wagering

Typically NZ$5–8 per spin. Exceed it once, including accidentally after changing stake mid-autoplay, and the operator may void the bonus and everything won from it. This is enforceable, it is the most common way players lose a legitimate win, and it is entirely avoidable by checking your stake after any change.

Game weighting

Pokies count 100%. Live blackjack and roulette usually 10% or zero. Some jackpot titles are excluded entirely. Staking NZ$100 on blackjack under 10% weighting contributes NZ$10 toward wagering, so ten times the turnover for the same progress.

Expiry

7 to 30 days typically. Clearing NZ$8,000 of turnover inside seven days at NZ$1 a spin means 8,000 spins, or roughly nine hours of continuous play. If the window cannot realistically be met, the offer's effective value is zero.

Wagering requirements: frequently asked questions

What does 40x wagering mean?

You must stake 40 times a specified base before withdrawing. On bonus only with a NZ$100 bonus that is NZ$4,000 of turnover. On deposit plus bonus after a NZ$100 deposit it is NZ$8,000, twice as much from the identical headline number. Finding which base applies is the single most important thing you can do before claiming an offer.

Is a bonus with high wagering worth taking?

Only if you were going to play that much anyway. At 96% RTP, clearing NZ$8,000 of turnover has an expected cost of NZ$320 to win a NZ$100 bonus. If that turnover was happening regardless, the bonus is free value on top. If the bonus is what makes you stake NZ$8,000 instead of NZ$200, it has cost you money.

Why do live casino games not count fully toward wagering?

Because live blackjack returns about 99.3% with basic strategy, which makes clearing a bonus far too cheap for the operator. Pokies at 96% cost you roughly 4% of turnover, giving a 40 times requirement a real expected cost. Blackjack would cost a fraction of that, so it is weighted at 10% or excluded.

Can I withdraw before finishing the wagering?

Not while the bonus is active, since any bonus-derived portion of the balance blocks the entire withdrawal. Most operators do let you forfeit the bonus, which cancels it and any winnings attributable to it, releasing your remaining cash. If you are well short of the requirement and want your money, forfeiting is often the correct decision.

Is 40x wagering good or bad?

It sits at the market median and is acceptable on bonus only, marginal on deposit plus bonus. On bonus only with a NZ$100 bonus it means NZ$4,000 of turnover, costing about NZ$160 in expected loss at 96% RTP to capture NZ$100. On deposit plus bonus it doubles to NZ$8,000 and NZ$320, which is poor value.

How do I calculate what a bonus really costs?

Multiply the wagering by its base to get required turnover, then multiply that by the house edge of what you will play — 4% on a standard pokie. A NZ$100 bonus at 25 times on bonus only costs NZ$100 of expected loss to clear, so it breaks even. The same bonus at 50 times on deposit plus bonus costs NZ$400 to capture NZ$100.

What does game weighting mean?

How much each game type counts toward clearing your wagering. Pokies almost always count 100%, live tables 10% or nothing. At 10% weighting, clearing a NZ$4,000 requirement on blackjack means staking NZ$40,000. It is not an administrative detail — it is the mechanism that stops you clearing a bonus on the game where you have the best odds.

Can I withdraw before finishing the wagering?

Not while the bonus is active, since any bonus-derived portion of the balance blocks the entire withdrawal. Most operators let you forfeit the bonus, cancelling it and any winnings attributable to it while releasing your remaining cash. If you are well short and want your money, forfeiting is often correct.

What is the difference between sticky and non-sticky bonuses?

A sticky bonus cannot itself be withdrawn — only winnings above it are yours, and the bonus amount is removed at cashout. A non-sticky bonus becomes real money once wagering completes. Non-sticky is better for you, and the distinction is usually in the terms rather than on the banner.

Do all games count toward wagering?

No. Beyond weighting, most operators publish an excluded games list — commonly the highest-RTP titles, jackpot pokies and sometimes bonus-buy features. Playing an excluded game with bonus funds contributes nothing and at some operators can void the bonus entirely. Read the list before you start.

Why is wagering applied to my deposit as well as the bonus?

Because the operator chose that base, and it doubles what you owe from an identical headline multiplier. It is one line in the terms and it is the single most consequential thing to check before claiming any offer. Wherever you have the choice, take the bonus-only version even if the headline number is smaller.
