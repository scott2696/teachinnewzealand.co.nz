Who can lawfully take a bet from New Zealand

TAB NZ only, through its TAB and Betcha brands. Legislation passed unanimously in 2025 made it unlawful for overseas-based operators to accept bets from inside New Zealand, extending a monopoly that had previously covered only physical venues to cover online betting as well.

TAB NZ is a statutory body operating under the Racing Industry Act, delivering its betting products in partnership with Entain, and regulated by the Department of Internal Affairs. Betting profits are directed to the racing industry and New Zealand sport by statute rather than to overseas shareholders.

What the monopoly costs you

We are not going to pretend this is costless. A single operator with no competitors has no pressure to sharpen its pricing.

Odds are not competitive

With no rival book to be undercut by, margins do not compress. On a two-way market you may be giving up a percentage point or two against what an open market would price.

Market depth is narrower

Large international books list obscure leagues and exotic prop markets that a domestic operator serving five million people cannot justify. If you bet niche, you will notice.

No promotional competition

Free bet arms races happen where operators compete. Here they largely do not.

What you get in exchange

A regulator who will take your complaint, New Zealand consumer law, funds that cannot be stranded offshore, statutory harm minimisation obligations, and revenue that stays in New Zealand racing and sport.

Whether that is a good trade is a political question, not one we can settle. What we can tell you is that the trade exists and that no offshore alternative currently gives you the left-hand column lawfully.

The practical risks, beyond legality: payment blocks and reversals that strand money mid-transfer, accounts closed without warning as operators withdraw from the New Zealand market, balances confiscated on exit, and no regulator with jurisdiction to help. These are not hypothetical — they are what happens as a market closes.

Sports betting sites NZ: frequently asked questions

What is the best sports betting site in New Zealand?

TAB NZ, because it is the only operator lawfully permitted to accept bets from people in New Zealand. Its Betcha brand offers a more modern app and deeper in-play markets if you bet mainly on sport rather than racing. Both are TAB NZ, both are regulated by the Department of Internal Affairs, and both give you recourse an offshore book cannot.

Are offshore betting sites illegal in New Zealand?

It is unlawful for them to accept bets from people in New Zealand. The prohibition binds the operator rather than the player, so placing a bet is not an offence for you. But you have no regulatory recourse if they refuse to pay, no New Zealand consumer protection, and increasing practical risk from payment blocks and accounts closed as operators exit the market.

Why does this site not list offshore sportsbooks?

Because we would be recommending something you cannot lawfully act on and that carries real risk of losing your money. Several casinos we review run a sportsbook, and we say on each review that the sportsbook is not lawfully available in New Zealand. That costs us commission. We think the accuracy is worth more.

Does the TAB monopoly mean worse odds?

Generally yes. Without competing books to undercut its pricing, margins do not compress the way they do in an open market, and market depth on niche leagues is narrower than a large international operator offers. The trade is a regulator with jurisdiction over your complaint, New Zealand consumer law, and betting revenue that stays in New Zealand racing and sport.
