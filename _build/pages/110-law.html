This page is written and fact-checked by Charlotte Wilson, who read law at Te Herenga Waka—Victoria University of Wellington, and every claim traces to a primary source listed at the bottom. It is general information about how the law applies to consumers, not advice on your circumstances. For advice, see a lawyer.

Is it legal to gamble online in New Zealand?

For the player, yes. The Gambling Act 2003 is written around those who conduct or promote gambling. It does not create an offence of participating in overseas online gambling, and no New Zealander has been prosecuted for placing a bet or a spin online. That was true before 2026 and remains true now.

What was missing was any regulation of the operators taking that money. New Zealanders were spending substantial sums at offshore casinos with no licensing, no harm minimisation requirements, no age verification standards enforceable here, and no regulator with jurisdiction. The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 addresses that.

The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026

The Act came into force on 1 May 2026. Its central mechanism is a capped licensing regime: up to 15 licences for online casino operators serving New Zealand, allocated by auction, each running three years with one renewal of a further five years. The Department of Internal Affairs is not obliged to issue all fifteen.

Advertising

From 1 May 2026 operators must not advertise online casino gambling services to New Zealand customers while unlicensed. Advertising remains prohibited while an application is under assessment, though the applicant may continue operating. Once a licence is granted, the operator may advertise in New Zealand subject to restrictions set under the Act.

This is why you may notice New Zealand-facing casino marketing becoming quieter through 2026 and 2027 — it is a legal requirement, not a change in appetite.

What this means for you as a player

Playing remains lawful

The Act regulates operators. It creates no offence for a person in New Zealand who plays at an online casino, licensed or otherwise.

Some sites will disappear

Operators that did not apply by 1 December 2026 must stop serving New Zealand players. If you hold a balance at one, withdraw it well before it exits.

Licensed sites will be materially safer

A licensee must satisfy the DIA on consumer protection and harm minimisation, and will be answerable to a New Zealand regulator. That is a step change from an offshore licence.

Fewer choices, better ones

Fifteen licensed operators is a smaller market than the dozens currently accessible. The trade is real oversight in place of self-regulation.

What the Act does not change

  • Sports and racing betting. Entirely separate and far stricter. TAB NZ, including Betcha, is the only operator lawfully permitted to accept bets from people in New Zealand, following 2025 legislation prohibiting offshore bookmakers from taking New Zealand bets. See online betting NZ.
  • Tax on winnings. Gambling winnings remain a non-taxable windfall for recreational players. See tax on gambling winnings.
  • Class 4 pub pokies. Still governed by the Gambling Act 2003, still capped at a NZ$2.50 stake and NZ$500 single-play prize under the Gambling (Harm Prevention and Minimisation) Regulations 2004. See pub pokies vs online.
  • Land-based casinos. SkyCity and the other physical venues operate under their existing casino licences, unaffected.
  • The age limit. 18 for online casino and for Class 4 gambling. Land-based casinos are 20.

Offshore gambling duty

Separate from licensing, New Zealand applies an offshore gambling duty to operators taking money from New Zealand residents. That duty increased from 12% to 16% in October 2025. It is levied on the operator rather than the player, but it feeds through into what operators can afford to offer — one reason bonus generosity in the New Zealand market has tightened.

NZ online casino law: frequently asked questions

Is online gambling legal in New Zealand?

For players, yes. The Gambling Act 2003 regulates those who conduct or promote gambling and creates no offence of participating in overseas online gambling. No New Zealander has been prosecuted for playing online. The operator side is now licensed under the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026, which came into force on 1 May 2026.

What is the penalty for online gambling in New Zealand?

There is none for the player. New Zealand law targets the provision of gambling, not participation in it. Penalties under the Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 apply to operators conducting online casino gambling without a licence or advertising in breach of the Act, not to the people who play.

How many online casino licences will New Zealand issue?

Up to 15, allocated by auction. Each licence runs three years and may be renewed once for a further five. The Department of Internal Affairs is not obliged to issue all fifteen, so the licensed market may end up smaller.

What happens to unlicensed casinos after December 2026?

Operators that had not applied for a licence by 1 December 2026 must stop conducting online casino gambling in New Zealand. Those that did apply may continue until their application is determined or 1 July 2027, whichever is earlier. If you hold a balance at a site that is exiting, withdraw it before it goes.

Can offshore casinos advertise in New Zealand?

Not while unlicensed. From 1 May 2026 operators must not advertise online casino gambling services to New Zealand customers unless licensed, and advertising is not permitted while an application is under assessment even though the operator may continue to take play. Once a licence is granted, advertising is allowed subject to restrictions set under the Act.

Is online sports betting covered by the same law?

No, and the two point in opposite directions. Sports and racing betting is governed by separate legislation which made it unlawful in 2025 for offshore operators to accept bets from people in New Zealand, leaving TAB NZ and its Betcha brand as the only lawful provider. Online casino was liberalised and licensed; sports betting was closed.
