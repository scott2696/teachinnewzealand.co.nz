The locked title problem

Free spins are almost always restricted to one pokie chosen by the operator. That choice is not random — it is usually a new release the studio is paying to promote, or a title where the operator runs a specific configuration.

Two consequences. First, check the deployed RTP before using them; it takes thirty seconds in the information panel and it is the difference between spins worth NZ$28.80 and spins worth NZ$28.20 in expectation. Second, the locked title's volatility decides your realistic outcome. Fifty spins on a very-high-volatility title will produce nothing about 70% of the time; fifty spins on a low-volatility title will produce a small win most of the time. Neither is better in expectation, but if there is a withdrawal cap you cannot exceed, low volatility is strictly the better fit.

How to play free spins properly

Read the cap first, then decide how to play

If withdrawable winnings are capped at NZ$100, your objective is to reach NZ$100 and clear the wagering — not to maximise the balance. Anything above the cap is decoration, and that single fact should change your title selection toward low volatility.

Check the locked title before you spin

Open its information panel and read the deployed RTP. It takes thirty seconds and it is the only part of the offer you can influence.

Stay under the max bet through the wagering

Usually NZ$5. Exceeding it once, including after an autoplay stake change, can void everything. Set it and leave it.

Withdraw the moment it clears

Bonus-derived balances left in an account get played back. That is the entire commercial logic of the reversal window.

Common free spins mistakes

Treating the spin count as the value

300 spins at NZ$0.10 is NZ$30 of stake. The headline count is the least informative number in the offer and it is the one on the banner.

Ignoring the locked title's RTP

Thirty seconds in the information panel. Two of the sixteen sites lock spins to a title running a reduced build.

Playing high volatility against a withdrawal cap

If winnings are capped at NZ$100, chasing a large win is pointless — you cannot take it out. Low volatility maximises your chance of reaching the cap and clearing the wagering.

Missing the separate expiry clocks

The spins and the wagering window frequently run on different timers, and the spins clock is often the shorter. Check both when you claim.
