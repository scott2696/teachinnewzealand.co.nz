Deposits always work — that is the direction the operator wants money moving, and it is engineered accordingly. Terms that let a legitimate win be voided

Look for clauses permitting cancellation of winnings for "irregular play", "bonus abuse" or "advantage play" without defining any of them. A max-bet rule expressed as a number is legitimate and enforceable — if you exceed NZ$5 a spin while wagering, you will lose that dispute and you should. An undefined discretionary clause is different: it is a mechanism for not paying, and it is why that filter is absolute rather than a scoring penalty.

Whether the licence number actually resolves

Every licensed operator prints a number in the footer. It should resolve on the licensor's public register. If it does not resolve, or the footer shows a logo with no number at all, that is disqualifying — and it is a check you can run yourself in thirty seconds before depositing anywhere, including at sites we have never looked at.

Anyone can answer "where are my free spins". The test is asking live chat, in writing, what the daily withdrawal ceiling is and whether progressive jackpot wins are exempt from it. Reading published RTP before you stake

  1. Open the game and find the information button

    An "i", a burger icon, or inside the settings cog — usually bottom-left or bottom-right of the game frame. It is present on every certified title.

  2. Go to the last page of the paytable

    The RTP statement is almost always in the final section, alongside the maximum win and the rules text.

  3. Compare against the studio's published figure

    Search the title plus the studio name plus "RTP". A material gap — 96.5% published, 94.0% in game — means the operator has deployed a reduced build.

  4. If no RTP is shown anywhere, treat that as the answer

    An operator running standard configurations has no reason to hide the number. HellSpin shows RTP on the game tile itself, which is the behaviour we would like to see everywhere.

How casino bonuses actually pay out

The multiplier and its base

The single most consequential term, and it is usually one line deep in the T&Cs. 40× on bonus only with a NZ$100 bonus is NZ$4,000 of turnover. The same 40× on deposit plus bonus after a NZ$100 deposit is NZ$8,000 — identical headline, double the obligation.

Game weighting

Pokies almost always count 100%. Live blackjack and roulette often count 10% or nothing at all. Staking NZ$100 on blackjack under 10% weighting contributes NZ$10 toward the requirement, so you would need ten times the turnover for the same progress. If tables are your game, most bonuses are close to worthless to you.

The maximum bet while wagering

Typically NZ$5–8 a spin. Exceed it once — including accidentally on autoplay after changing stake — and the operator may void the bonus and everything won from it. This is the most common way players lose a legitimate win, it is entirely avoidable, and you will not win the dispute.

Win caps and expiry

No-deposit offers usually cap withdrawable winnings, often at around NZ$100 regardless of what you win. Most bonuses expire in 7 to 30 days. Clearing NZ$8,000 of turnover inside seven days at NZ$1 a spin means 8,000 spins, or roughly nine hours of continuous play. An offer you cannot realistically clear in the window has an effective value of zero.

Our rule after pricing all sixteen: a smaller bonus at 25–35× on bonus only beats a headline package at 50× on deposit plus bonus, every time. And if you want to keep the ability to withdraw at any moment, decline the bonus — every site here permits this, usually with a checkbox at deposit or a forfeit option in your account.

Encryption, verification and what happens to your documents

Every site here runs TLS across the whole session, which is table stakes rather than a feature. The parts worth understanding are the ones involving your identity documents.

Why casinos ask for ID

Anti-money-laundering obligations require a licensed operator to establish who you are before paying out. It is a legal requirement, not a delay tactic, and it applies at every legitimate site. The mistake most players make is leaving it until the moment they win.

What you will be asked for

Photo ID (passport or driver licence), a recent proof of address no more than three months old, and sometimes proof of the payment method — a card photo with the middle digits obscured, or an e-wallet screenshot. On large or unusual wins you may face a source-of-funds request: payslips or bank statements showing where your deposit money came from. Intrusive, legitimate, and unavoidable.

Tiered verification, and why it matters

Some operators verify in tiers — light checks below a threshold, full document review above it. Kingdom uses NZ$3,000, Smash NZ$2,000. For anyone withdrawing modest amounts regularly, that difference is worth more than any bonus on this page, because it is the difference between a cashout that clears this evening and one that clears when a reviewer gets to it.

Does New Zealand law permit offshore casino accounts?

It is not an offence for a person in New Zealand to play at an overseas online casino. The Gambling Act 2003 is written around those who conduct or promote gambling, and no New Zealander has been prosecuted for placing a bet or a spin online.

What is changing is the operator side. The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 came into force on 1 May 2026 and creates up to 15 New Zealand licences, allocated by auction, each running three years with one five-year renewal. Operators that had not applied by 1 December 2026 must stop serving New Zealand players; those that applied may continue until their application is determined or 1 July 2027, whichever is earlier.

Sports betting is separate and stricter. Since 2025, TAB NZ — including its Betcha brand — has been the only operator lawfully permitted to accept racing and sports bets from people in New Zealand. What happens when an operator exits the New Zealand market

Up to 15 New Zealand licences exist. There are considerably more than 15 operators currently serving New Zealanders. The arithmetic means a number of sites will exit during 2027, either because they did not apply or because they were not successful.

Three practical consequences:

  • Do not hold a float. Withdraw winnings rather than leaving a balance on a site. This was always sensible; during a market exit it is essential.
  • Watch for a shrinking cashier. Payment methods disappearing one by one is the earliest visible signal that an operator is winding down a market.
  • There is no compensation scheme. None of the offshore licensors covering NZ-facing operators runs anything comparable to the UK or Malta. An online casino balance is not protected savings, and it never was.

Keeping play under control

Every site on this page offers deposit limits, loss limits, session limits, reality checks, time-outs and self-exclusion. Almost nobody turns them on. The deposit limit is the one that matters, because of an asymmetry the others lack: tightening it takes effect immediately, loosening it takes 24 to 72 hours at any reputable operator. That delay puts a cooling-off period between an impulse and the money, which is exactly when protection is needed.

The gap worth knowing about: in a New Zealand venue, Multi-Venue Exclusion lets you exclude yourself from every participating Class 4 venue in a region with a single request. Online self-exclusion stops at one operator's door and is not portable. If you need a broad block, several New Zealand banks let you block gambling merchant categories from within their app, which covers every operator at once including ones you have not signed up to. Do I pay tax on casino winnings in New Zealand?

Not as a recreational player. The exceptions are gambling carried on as a business, interest earned once the money is banked, and cryptoasset disposals, which have their own rules.
