The four numbers

Number 1

Value per spin

Usually NZ$0.10 to NZ$0.20. 300 spins at NZ$0.10 is NZ$30 of stake, not NZ$300 of value. Multiply before you get excited.

Number 2

Wagering on winnings

Typically 35× to 50× on whatever the spins produce. Win NZ$20 at 40× and you owe NZ$800 of turnover before withdrawing.

Number 3

Win cap

Common on no-deposit spins at around NZ$100. Win NZ$600, clear the wagering, withdraw NZ$100.

Number 4

The locked title's RTP

Spins are restricted to one pokie. Open its information panel. Free spins on a 94% build are worth noticeably less than the same spins on a 96.5% one.

Run those four numbers and most headline free-spin offers resolve to NZ$10–25 of genuine expected value. That is a fair thing to be given and a poor reason to choose a casino.

The types, ranked

No-wagering free spins — take these

Winnings paid as withdrawable cash with no playthrough. Values are modest but the value is real, and the entire cost of an ordinary bonus sits in the wagering you are being spared. Uncommon; worth switching sites for.

Recurring weekly spins — the quiet winner

A drop that lands every week from a small qualifying deposit is worth far more over a year than a one-off welcome package. Lucky Circus runs the most reliable one we track: a Monday drop qualifying from NZ$20.

Deposit free spins — usually fine

Attached to a deposit you were making anyway, typically at similar wagering to a cash bonus. Reasonable value if the locked title is a good one.

No-deposit free spins — try, expect little

Free to claim, so there is no reason not to. The win cap and high wagering mean the realistic outcome is a pleasant hour. See no deposit bonuses.

Spins on an unnamed or reduced-RTP title — check first

If the offer does not name the title, or names one you find running a 94% build, the spins are worth materially less than the headline suggests.

The four numbers, in the order to check them

  1. Value per spin

    Usually NZ$0.10–0.20. 300 spins at NZ$0.10 is NZ$30 of stake, not NZ$300 of value. Multiply before you get excited — this alone deflates most headline offers by 90%.

    Typically 35–50× on whatever the spins produce. Check whether it applies to the winnings or to a notional value of the spins themselves, which is worse and is used more often than you would expect.

    Common on no-deposit spins at around NZ$100. Once you know the cap, the correct way to play changes entirely — there is no value in building a balance you cannot take out.

  4. The locked title's deployed RTP

    Open its information panel. Spins on a 94% build are worth materially less than the same spins on a 96.5% one, and two of the sixteen sites lock spins to a title I found running a reduced configuration.

The locked title problem

Free spins are almost always restricted to one pokie chosen by the operator. That choice is not random — it is usually a new release the studio is paying to promote, or a title where the operator runs a specific configuration.

Two consequences. First, check the deployed RTP before using them; it takes thirty seconds in the information panel and it is the difference between spins worth NZ$28.80 and spins worth NZ$28.20 in expectation. Second, the locked title's volatility decides your realistic outcome. Fifty spins on a very-high-volatility title will produce nothing about 70% of the time; fifty spins on a low-volatility title will produce a small win most of the time. Neither is better in expectation, but if there is a withdrawal cap you cannot exceed, low volatility is strictly the better fit.

How to play free spins properly

Read the cap first, then decide how to play

If withdrawable winnings are capped at NZ$100, your objective is to reach NZ$100 and clear the wagering — not to maximise the balance. Anything above the cap is decoration, and that single fact should change your title selection toward low volatility.

Check the locked title before you spin

Open its information panel and read the deployed RTP. It takes thirty seconds and it is the only part of the offer you can influence.

Stay under the max bet through the wagering

Usually NZ$5. Exceeding it once, including after an autoplay stake change, can void everything. Set it and leave it.

Withdraw the moment it clears

Bonus-derived balances left in an account get played back. That is the entire commercial logic of the reversal window.

Common free spins mistakes

Treating the spin count as the value

300 spins at NZ$0.10 is NZ$30 of stake. The headline count is the least informative number in the offer and it is the one on the banner.

Ignoring the locked title's RTP

Thirty seconds in the information panel. Two of the sixteen sites lock spins to a title running a reduced build.

Playing high volatility against a withdrawal cap

If winnings are capped at NZ$100, chasing a large win is pointless — you cannot take it out. Low volatility maximises your chance of reaching the cap and clearing the wagering.

Missing the separate expiry clocks

The spins and the wagering window frequently run on different timers, and the spins clock is often the shorter. Check both when you claim.

Free spins: frequently asked questions

What are free spins worth in real terms?

Multiply the spin count by the value per spin, then discount heavily for wagering and any win cap. 300 spins at NZ$0.10 is NZ$30 of stake, which at 96% RTP returns about NZ$28.80 on average, and 40 times wagering on those winnings plus a NZ$100 cap reduces the realistic expected value to somewhere around NZ$10 to NZ$25.

Do I have to wager free spin winnings?

Almost always, typically at 35 to 50 times what the spins produced. The exception is a no-wagering offer, where winnings are immediately withdrawable. Those are uncommon and genuinely valuable, because the entire cost of a normal bonus sits in the playthrough obligation you are being spared.

Can I choose which pokie to use free spins on?

Rarely. Spins are usually locked to one title chosen by the operator, often a new release they want played or one where they run a specific configuration. Open that title's information panel and check the RTP before using the spins, because free spins on a 94% build are worth noticeably less than the same spins on a 96.5% one.

One where winnings are paid as immediately withdrawable cash with no playthrough requirement. Check it is genuinely no wagering rather than 'no wagering on the spins, 35x on the winnings', which is a very different and much worse thing. Values are usually modest but the value is real.

Are free spins better than a deposit match?

Often, at small deposit sizes. Free spins carry wagering only on what they produce, whereas a deposit match applies wagering to the bonus and sometimes to your deposit as well. A 50-spin no-wagering offer beats a NZ$100 match at 50 times on deposit plus bonus by a wide margin.

Why did my free spins expire before I used them?

Spins and the wagering window often run on separate clocks, and the spins clock is usually the shorter of the two — sometimes 24 hours. Check both durations when you claim. Unused spins are almost never reinstated, and support will point you to the term.

What is the max bet rule during free spins wagering?

Usually NZ$5 per spin once you are playing through the winnings. Exceeding it once, including accidentally after an autoplay stake change, can void the bonus and everything won from it. It is a stated numeric rule and it is enforceable, so set the stake and leave it.
